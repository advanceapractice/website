## 0. Method, evidence base, and limitations — read this first

**What I actually did.** I ran live searches on every money term and niche term listed below and recorded the real SERP composition — who ranks, what URL, what the page format is, and what the title/framing signals about content strategy. Every URL cited in this report is one I saw returned in a live search result on 2026-09-14.

**Hard constraints that shaped the evidence.**

1. **advanceapractice.com was not fetched.** Per instruction, the client site was never requested. Everything said about the client's own pages is inference from third-party SERP appearances only. One observation: the client's homepage *does* surface in the Portland local SERP with the title `Behavioral Health Billing, Credentialing & Practice Operations | AdvanceAPractice`, and appears in two third-party "top Portland billing companies" roundups. That is the only first-hand data point I have on the client's current indexation.
2. **WebFetch is blocked org-wide in this environment.** I attempted page-level fetches on `therathink.com`, `simplepractice.com`, `choosingtherapy.com`, `psychiatricbilling.com`, `relias.com`, `aapc.com`, and `cms.gov`. **All returned `EGRESS_BLOCKED`.** I could not retrieve a single external page body. Therefore all depth/format assessments below are inferred from SERP titles, URL structure, result descriptions, and the synthesized answer content returned with each search — not from reading the pages. **Where I characterize a competitor page's depth, treat it as a structural inference, not a verified word count.** Flagged as `[inferred]` throughout.
3. **No keyword tool.** I have no access to Ahrefs/Semrush/GKP. **There are zero search-volume numbers and zero KD scores in this report, and none should be added later by copying from here.** Every difficulty rating is my judgment, with its basis stated.

**How I estimate difficulty without tools.** Each rating is derived from four observable SERP properties:
- **Who occupies the top slots** (institutional / high-DR platform / niche specialist / content mill).
- **Homogeneity** — if 8 of 10 results are near-identical templated pages from unrelated RCM vendors, the term is *commercially proven but cheap to enter and hard to hold*.
- **Presence of an incumbent specialist** — a single niche site holding multiple positions (TheraThink, Behave Health) signals an entrenched topical authority.
- **Whether a *maintained, dated* asset exists** — a "2026 payer-by-payer table" that someone clearly updates is much harder to displace than a dated blog post.

**One methodological proxy I will use explicitly, because it is defensible.** Offshore RCM content mills allocate production budget to queries with demonstrated commercial traffic value. **The density of near-identical mill pages on a query is an observable proxy for commercial demand** — it does not measure volume, but it does tell you someone's analytics says the term is worth money. I use "mill density" as a demand signal and say so each time.

**Difficulty scale used (all judgments):**

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Low** | Top 10 is help-center docs, payer PDFs, forum posts, or thin vendor pages. No entrenched specialist. A genuinely good page wins within ~2 quarters. |
| **Moderate** | Mix of mid-tier vendors and one or two credible specialists. Winnable with superior depth + real author authority + maintenance. |
| **High** | An entrenched niche specialist holds multiple positions, or 3+ high-DR platforms (SimplePractice, Headway, Relias) occupy the page. Needs a differentiated angle, not a better version of the same page. |
| **Very High** | Head commercial term owned by high-DR national RCM firms and platform brands, plus heavy paid competition. Not winnable organically for a small site on a reasonable horizon. |

### 1.3 E-E-A-T read: the client's single largest untapped asset

Across roughly 150 ranking URLs observed, I saw **almost no named human authors with verifiable billing credentials.** The mills are anonymous. The platforms byline a brand. The niche challengers byline nothing.

The client has: **a named founder (Ryan Berg), two real certifications (CPB, CMOM), 16+ years, a physical market (Portland OR), and — critically — first-hand operational data from actual submissions, denials, and enrollments.**

That is a category-of-one E-E-A-T position in this vertical, and it is currently being wasted. Google's helpful-content systems and AI Overview citation selection both disproportionately reward exactly the thing the mills structurally cannot fake: **first-hand experience with dated, specific, verifiable particulars.** This is the strategic center of the whole plan, not a garnish.

## 2. Query intent map

### 2.1 The three layers

**Layer 1 — High-intent commercial (ready to hire).**
`mental health billing services` · `behavioral health billing company` · `psychiatry billing services` · `therapy billing services` · `credentialing services for therapists` · `behavioral health revenue cycle management` · `payer enrollment services` · `{service} + {city/state}`
**SERP shape:** national RCM firms with long templated service pages + geo-programmatic mills + heavy paid competition.
**Verdict for a small site: mostly unwinnable head-on. Winnable only in the geo/segment long tail** (`behavioral health billing services Oregon`, `PMHNP billing services`, `group therapy practice billing company`).

**Layer 2 — Commercial investigation (comparing / pricing / deciding).**
`what percentage do mental health billing companies charge` · `mental health billing services cost` · `Headway vs Alma vs Grow Therapy` · `Headway vs private practice` · `best credentialing services for therapists` · `should I hire a biller or a platform` · `in-house vs outsourced billing therapy practice`
**SERP shape:** mixed. Roundups (ChoosingTherapy), platform self-serving comparisons (Headway comparing itself to Alma), **and small independent sites winning on honesty** (practiceharbor.com's "The Honest Math", therapydial.com's payout-rate comparisons).
**Verdict: this is the highest-ROI winnable layer.** Buyers here are 1–2 clicks from a contract, incumbents are conflicted, and an independent operator has a structural credibility advantage no platform can match.

**Layer 3 — Informational / problem-aware.**
Denial codes · CAQH steps · payer timelines · CPT codes · telehealth POS · superbills · EAP · ERA/EFT · no-show policy · state Medicaid enrollment
**SERP shape:** saturated by mills on the generic slices (CPT codes, denial codes, timely filing); **genuinely weak on the operationally specific slices** (EAP carrier-by-carrier, ERA/EFT by payer, state Medicaid mechanics, PMHNP scope).
**Verdict: mixed — must be segmented, not attacked wholesale.** Attacking "CPT 90837" generically is a loss. Attacking "which payers audit 90837 and what documentation survives it" is a win.

### 2.2 Which layer the client is losing — and why

**Diagnosis: the client is losing Layer 3, and that is almost certainly the traffic decline.**

Reasoning, stated as inference (the client's analytics were not available and the site was not fetched):

1. **Layer 3 is the only layer that produces large organic volume for a firm this size.** Layer 1 head terms were never realistically won by a small founder-led site — so a *major* decline cannot be explained by losing them.
2. **Layer 3 is exactly where the mill flood landed.** The searches show 8–10 near-identical, year-stamped competitors on nearly every generic informational term (`CO-197`, `timely filing limits`, `CPT 90837`, `mental health CPT codes`). That volume of production is recent — the pervasive "2026" stamping indicates an active, rolling refresh cycle. A modest blog of evergreen posts gets buried by a competitor set that re-stamps and re-publishes continuously.
3. **Layer 3 is the layer most compressed by AI Overviews.** Every search I ran returned a synthesized answer assembled from mid-tier sources. Definitional Layer-3 queries ("what is CAQH", "what is a superbill", "what does CO-197 mean") now resolve without a click. **Traffic to generic definitional content in this niche is structurally impaired and will not come back.** Chasing it is chasing a shrinking pool.
4. **Programmatic challengers have taken specific high-value Layer-3 ground, including the client's home turf.** Bomi Health ranks for Oregon Medicaid credentialing *including CCO-level detail (CareOregon, Health Share)* — a Portland-based firm losing Portland-relevant Medicaid queries to a programmatic out-of-state challenger is a concrete, correctable loss.

**Which layer is winnable for a small expert site:**

| Layer | Winnable? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| L1 head commercial | **No** — do not invest | DR wall + paid competition. Defend the pages, don't chase the term |
| L1 segment/geo long tail | **Yes** | `PMHNP billing services`, `behavioral health billing Oregon`, `group practice credentialing` — mills are thin here, specificity wins |
| **L2 commercial investigation** | **Yes — highest priority** | Incumbents are conflicted; independence is the moat; buyers are near-transaction |
| L3 generic (codes, denial codes, timely filing) | **No** — deprioritize | Saturated + AI-Overview-compressed. Build only as cluster support |
| **L3 operationally specific** | **Yes — highest volume opportunity** | EAP by carrier, ERA/EFT by payer, state Medicaid, PMHNP scope, payer-specific audit behavior. Mills cannot fake first-hand payer knowledge |

**The strategic sentence:** stop competing on *what a code means* and start owning *what a specific payer actually does* — because the first is commoditized and AI-summarized, and the second requires experience the competition doesn't have.

### 5.2 What the client should build, ranked by link/citation leverage

1. **Behavioral Health Credentialing Timeline Tracker** *(flagship)* — payer × state × provider-license, with observed-vs-published timelines, an explicit "last verified" date per row, and a changelog. **Nobody in behavioral health has this.** The generic-medical versions are the proof of demand.
2. **Original data report: "What behavioral health claims actually get denied for"** — drawn from the client's own book of business, anonymized and aggregated. **This is the highest-leverage asset available and the only one no competitor can replicate.** The niche currently repeats one secondhand statistic ("BH denial rate is 5–10% higher"); a primary source would become the cited source. Publish annually.
3. **EAP Carrier Matrix** — code, modifier, auth requirement, form requirement, member-ID quirk, per carrier. Fills the clearest gap found.
4. **ERA/EFT Enrollment Tracker** — enrollment route, portal/clearinghouse, typical turnaround, per payer.
5. **"Platform vs. your own contracts" calculator** — session volume × rate differential × billing cost → annual delta. Interactive tools of this shape are the most-shared format in practitioner communities, and it converts directly into the credentialing service.
6. **Appeal letter template pack** (denial-reason-specific, provider-facing) — the observed template content is consumer-facing; the provider-facing gap is open.
7. **Parity/NQTL appeal guide** — low traffic, high authority. This is the asset that earns .org and state-association links.
8. **Downloadables:** superbill template, no-show policy template, credentialing document checklist, BH taxonomy reference card.

### 5.3 Getting cited in AI Overviews

Every search in this study returned a synthesized answer built from multiple mid-tier sources. Observed selection patterns worth engineering for:

- **Direct-answer-first.** Synthesis favored pages whose lead paragraph resolves the query outright.
- **Specific numbers with attribution.** "90837 ≈ $167 non-facility POS 10 vs ≈ $125 POS 02" and "clean PECOS applications averaged 41 days" were both pulled verbatim — **concrete figures get lifted; vague prose does not.**
- **Tables.** Payer-by-payer tables were repeatedly the source of synthesized comparisons.
- **Explicit dating.** "Last verified" and year-stamping correlated strongly with inclusion.
- **Unique facts.** The most-quoted lines were ones available from only one source. **First-party data is the most reliable path to AI citation** — which routes back to asset #2 above.

## 6. Topic-cluster architecture

### 6.1 Model

A **four-quadrant hub-and-spoke**, with an explicit commercial bridge between the informational and service sides:

```
        MONEY PAGES                  AUDIENCE PAGES
   (what we do — convert)        (who we serve — qualify)
              \                        /
               \                      /
                +--  BRIDGE PAGES  --+
                  (diagnose → route)
                         |
                  RESOURCE PILLARS
                  (5 topical hubs)
                         |
                   CLUSTER PAGES
                         |
                TOOLS & DATA ASSETS
                (links + citations)
```

**Rule 1 — Clusters never link to the homepage as their commercial CTA.** Each cluster links *up* to its resource pillar and *across* to exactly one bridge page. Bridge pages carry the commercial handoff. This prevents the "every blog post CTAs to /contact" pattern that dilutes intent and kills conversion tracking.

**Rule 2 — Every service page must be supported by at least 5 cluster pages** that link to it with varied, descriptive anchors. This is how a small site manufactures the topical authority that DR alone can't buy.

**Rule 3 — Tools and data assets sit outside the cluster tree** and are linked from every relevant pillar and cluster page. They are link magnets; they should be the most-internally-linked URLs on the site.
